# Scientific English writing-quality scorecard

Use this scorecard consistently across all candidate papers. Evaluate the actual text, not the journal's reputation or the perceived background of the authors.

## Paper-level scorecard

| Dimension | Points | Questions |
|---|---:|---|
| Academic vocabulary precision | 20 | Are terms, verbs, nouns, and modifiers precise and contextually appropriate? |
| Syntax and sentence control | 20 | Are sentences grammatically controlled, varied where useful, and easy to parse? |
| Paragraph logic and argumentation | 20 | Does the paragraph progress coherently from claim to evidence and interpretation? |
| Natural academic expression | 15 | Are collocations, prepositions, and verb patterns natural and accurate? |
| Scientific reasoning and claim calibration | 15 | Does the text distinguish observations, results, interpretations, hypotheses, and conclusions? |
| Clarity and concision | 10 | Is the writing readable, economical, and free from avoidable redundancy? |
| **Total** | **100** | |

### Scoring anchors

For each dimension, use the full range only when justified:
- **Excellent:** consistently precise and effective; very few issues.
- **Good:** clear and reliable, with occasional weaknesses.
- **Adequate:** understandable but recurring imprecision or awkwardness.
- **Weak:** frequent problems that make it unsuitable as a primary writing model.

Do not overvalue sentence complexity. Simple, direct, well-calibrated writing can receive the highest score.

### A/B/C classification

- A: 85–100.
- B: 75–84.
- C: below 75.
- Not assessable: insufficient source text.

A/B/C labels are internal instructional categories, not external journal or language-proficiency ratings.

## Author-associated writing profile

This is separate from the paper score.

| Evidence profile | Criteria |
|---|---|
| Strong | At least two high-quality papers (three or more preferred) by the same first/corresponding author, plus documented drafting contribution in one or more papers |
| Moderate | Repeated high-quality papers or documented writing contribution, but not both |
| Limited | Only one paper or author-role information without specific writing contribution |
| Insufficient | Too little comparable text or contribution evidence |

Always note:
- author role in each paper;
- CRediT wording exactly as published;
- coauthor overlap;
- which sections were available;
- possible editing and copyediting effects.

Never state that the author personally wrote every sentence. Use "author-associated writing evidence" and explain the limitations.
