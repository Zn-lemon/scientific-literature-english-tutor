# Literature discovery, writing-quality evaluation, and 20-paper corpus

This module builds a high-quality English-language writing corpus matched to the learner's research direction. The paper's actual writing quality is the primary language-learning criterion. Author nationality and native-speaker status are not selection criteria.

## 1. Research-profile card

Before searching, create a compact profile:

| Field | Learner profile |
|---|---|
| Main discipline | ... |
| Subfield | ... |
| Organism / model | ... |
| Research question | ... |
| Genes / pathways / phenotypes | ... |
| Methods / analysis | ... |
| Adjacent topics allowed | ... |

Use known conversation context where relevant, but show the inferred profile for confirmation if it materially affects paper selection.

## 2. Journal priority

1. Cell, Nature, Science.
2. PNAS, Nature Communications, and other highly relevant journals verified as JCR Q1.
3. Other relevant SCIE journals verified as JCR Q1.

JCR quartile depends on edition and subject category. Always report both. Never substitute Chinese Academy of Sciences (CAS) quartile for JCR quartile without clearly labeling the difference. Journal prestige is a prioritization signal, not proof of writing quality.

## 3. Paper-level English writing-quality rubric

Score the paper's actual accessible text on a 100-point scale:

| Dimension | Points | Evaluation focus |
|---|---:|---|
| Academic vocabulary precision | 20 | Precise terms and lexical choices |
| Syntax and sentence control | 20 | Clear sentence construction and clause control |
| Paragraph logic and argumentation | 20 | Coherent progression of claims and evidence |
| Natural academic expression | 15 | Collocations, prepositions, verb patterns, natural phrasing |
| Scientific reasoning and claim calibration | 15 | Distinguishing observation, result, interpretation, hypothesis, and conclusion |
| Clarity and concision | 10 | Readability, economy, and lack of unnecessary complexity |
| **Total** | **100** | |

Classification:
- **A (85–100):** excellent writing model; primary language-learning material.
- **B (75–84):** high-quality writing model; suitable for the core corpus.
- **C (below 75):** supplementary scientific reference, not a primary imitation model.
- **Not assessable:** insufficient full text or source quality; do not invent a score.

These are internal instructional ratings, not standardized English proficiency scores. Do not equate complexity, long sentences, rare vocabulary, or journal impact with writing quality.

When accessible, evaluate representative sections: Abstract, Introduction, Results, and Discussion/Conclusions. State which sections were actually assessed. Separate writing quality from scientific novelty and journal prestige.

## 4. Author role and writing-contribution evidence

Verify first-author and corresponding-author roles from the publisher record or article itself.

Inspect the CRediT author-contribution statement when available. Give particular attention to:
- **Writing – original draft**
- **Writing – review & editing**

Use the following evidence labels:

| Label | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Strong | Verified first/corresponding role plus explicit Writing – original draft or equivalent documented drafting responsibility |
| Moderate | Verified role plus Writing – review & editing, repeated strong publications, or other relevant writing evidence, but primary drafting is not established |
| Limited / unclear | Role is verified but no specific writing contribution is documented |
| Unverified | Role or contribution information cannot be confirmed |

Important limitations:
- CRediT is a declared contribution taxonomy; it does not prove one author wrote every sentence.
- Coauthors, professional editors, language-editing services, and journal copyediting may affect the final text.
- Do not infer individual English ability from one publication.
- Do not use author nationality, name, country of employment, or presumed native language as a proxy for writing quality.
- Native-speaker status is not required and should not be researched for this workflow.

Use cautious wording such as "documented drafting contribution" or "author-associated writing evidence", not "proven individual writing ability".

## 5. Compare multiple papers by the same author

When feasible, compare at least two papers by the same first or corresponding author; three or more provide stronger evidence of consistency.

Assess comparable sections for:
- vocabulary precision;
- syntax and sentence clarity;
- paragraph-level argument structure;
- collocation and naturalness;
- scientific claim calibration;
- concision and readability.

Record:
- author's role in each paper;
- writing contribution evidence in each paper;
- paper-level writing score and grade;
- coauthor overlap;
- sections available for assessment;
- possible editorial or language-editing effects.

Author-associated profile:
- **Strong:** repeated high-quality papers plus documented drafting contribution in one or more papers.
- **Moderate:** repeated high-quality papers or documented writing contribution, but not both.
- **Limited:** one high-quality paper or role information only.
- **Insufficient evidence:** too few comparable papers or too little contribution information.

This is an evidence profile, not a definitive measurement of an individual's English proficiency. Do not exclude an excellent paper solely because its CRediT statement is missing.

## 6. Suggested discovery queries

Build queries from combinations of:
- model organism or system;
- biological question;
- gene / pathway / phenotype;
- method or analytical approach;
- journal names or JCR Q1 filter.

Templates:

```text
("[organism]" OR "[model system]") AND ("[research question]" OR "[pathway]")
("[gene family]" OR "[biological process]") AND ("genome evolution" OR "comparative genomics")
("[method]" AND "[organism]") AND ("Nature" OR "Science" OR "Cell" OR "PNAS")
```

Use broad queries for discovery, then narrow them. Include accepted synonyms and taxonomic names.

## 7. Verification table

| Rank | Title / year | Journal | JCR year/category/quartile | DOI | Article type | Topic fit | Writing score / grade | First author | Corresponding author | CRediT / writing evidence | Author-associated profile |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Verify title, author order, roles, journal, year, DOI, article type, and publisher page. Prefer publisher pages and article PDFs for text and contribution statements. Use JCR or an authoritative institutional database for quartile verification.

## 8. Select and sequence the 20-paper corpus

Classify papers as:
- direct match;
- adjacent field;
- methods / analytical model.

A starting balance of 12 / 6 / 2 may be used if supported by real candidates, but do not force it. Prioritize relevance, journal tier, and writing score.

Prefer A- and B-grade papers as the 20-paper language-learning corpus. C-grade papers may be listed separately as scientific background references. Do not force an exact A/B quota. If fewer than 20 A/B-grade papers meet the relevance and journal criteria, report the shortfall and ask whether to broaden the adjacent fields or journal scope. Do not quietly lower the writing-quality threshold.

After user approval, sequence papers from foundational to specialized. For each paper, specify:
- why it was selected;
- its writing score and grade;
- which sections were assessed;
- what language skills it can teach;
- what is known about first/corresponding-author writing contributions;
- the order in which it will be studied;
- which stage exam follows each section.

## 9. Search transparency and copyright

Include search date, sources searched, query concepts, number screened, inclusion/exclusion rules, writing rubric, and verification gaps.

If full text is not supplied by the user, do not reproduce long passages. Provide brief excerpts, citations, DOI/publisher links, and ask the user to supply the PDF for full sentence-by-sentence analysis.
