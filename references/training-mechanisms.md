# Three mandatory training mechanisms

The goal is to move the learner from understanding a source sentence to independently producing accurate academic English. Use the source paper as the teaching anchor, but do not copy its content into new examples.

## 1. Academic near-synonym discrimination

### Select useful contrasts

Choose only the contrasts relevant to the current paragraph. Examples:

| Contrast | Main distinction to teach |
|---|---|
| show / demonstrate / reveal | General presentation, evidence-backed demonstration, and making something previously unknown apparent |
| suggest / indicate / imply | Different ways of signaling inference and evidence strength; context matters |
| identify / determine / characterize | Recognizing an entity, establishing a value or outcome, and describing its properties |
| increase / expand / enhance | Quantitative rise, growth in scope or size, and improvement in degree or quality |
| affect / influence / regulate | Broad effect, influence, and controlled biological processes |
| cause / result in / contribute to | Direct causation, outcome relation, and partial contribution |
| observe / detect / discover | Seeing or recording, finding evidence of, and identifying something previously unknown |

Do not teach these as rigid universal rankings. Explain how the surrounding evidence and sentence construction determine the appropriate choice.

### Teaching sequence

1. Show the source word in context.
2. Explain its meaning, part of speech, argument strength, grammar pattern, and collocations.
3. Compare two or more plausible alternatives.
4. Explain why an alternative would be less suitable in this sentence.
5. Ask 2–3 short multiple-choice or fill-in questions when appropriate.
6. Wait for the learner's response.
7. Explain the answer and each distractor.
8. Add recurring confusions to the learner's error log.

### Example question format

**Teaching example — not from the source paper**

The results ______ that gene duplication may have contributed to the expansion of this gene family.

A. prove  
B. suggest  
C. guarantee  
D. ensure

Do not display the answer until the learner attempts the question.

## 2. Chinglish diagnosis and revision

### Task design

After a paragraph, create 2–3 short practice items based on its scientific topic and language patterns. Each item should include:

1. A Chinese scientific statement.
2. A deliberately awkward or error-prone English draft.
3. An instruction asking the learner to revise it independently.

Clearly mark the draft as a teaching example, not a sentence from the source.

### Feedback dimensions

After the learner responds, evaluate:

- grammatical accuracy;
- vocabulary precision;
- collocation and preposition use;
- sentence structure and information flow;
- scientific logic and accuracy;
- claim strength and appropriate qualification;
- naturalness in academic English.

### Three revision levels

Where useful, provide:

| Version | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Basic correct | Fixes grammar and meaning while remaining direct |
| Recommended academic | Natural, precise, and suitable for a research manuscript |
| Advanced alternative | Offers a different mature structure without adding unsupported claims |

Do not assume the advanced alternative is always preferable. Explain when the basic version is stronger because it is clearer.

### Error log

Record:
- learner's original sentence;
- error category;
- corrected version;
- reason for the correction;
- reusable rule or collocation;
- whether the same error has appeared before.

Separate language errors from scientific reasoning errors.

## 3. Active imitation and writing transfer

### Sentence-level imitation

1. Select 1–2 useful source patterns.
2. Explain syntax, vocabulary, rhetorical purpose, and logic.
3. Abstract the structure with X/Y/Z placeholders.
4. Give a new research scenario.
5. Ask the learner to write independently.
6. Do not reveal a complete model answer before the learner responds.
7. Assess grammar, academic vocabulary, collocation, logic, and scientific accuracy.
8. Provide a recommended revision and explain the transfer from the source pattern.

### Suitable research contexts

When relevant to the learner, use topics such as:
- moss and plant genome features;
- tandemly arrayed genes and gene-family expansion;
- photosynthesis-related genes;
- LTR retrotransposons and genome evolution;
- homologous recombination and DNA repair;
- plant stress adaptation;
- transcriptional regulation;
- plant metabolism and biosynthesis.

These are practice contexts only. Do not imply that the source paper demonstrated a new result in these areas unless it actually did.

### Paragraph-level transfer

At the end of a subsection, ask the learner to write a short original scientific paragraph using the patterns learned. Evaluate:

| Dimension | Score |
|---|---:|
| Grammar | 20 |
| Academic vocabulary | 20 |
| Collocation and naturalness | 20 |
| Logical organization | 20 |
| Scientific accuracy | 20 |
| **Total** | **100** |

Provide:
- the learner's original version;
- issues and explanations;
- a corrected version;
- a recommended academic version;
- comparison with the source pattern;
- strengths worth retaining;
- the next improvement target.

## Workload and pacing

- Do not interrupt every sentence with a large quiz.
- Explain each sentence first, then place the exercises after the paragraph.
- Use 2–3 questions per mechanism when the paragraph supports them; reduce the number if the learner is overloaded.
- Increase difficulty only when the learner demonstrates mastery.
- Revisit recurring errors in later paragraphs.
- The learner must attempt answers before receiving solutions.
