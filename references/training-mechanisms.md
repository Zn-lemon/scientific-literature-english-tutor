# Training mechanisms, error codes and practice load

Scope: the three training mechanisms (near-synonym discrimination, Chinglish diagnosis and revision, active imitation), the error-code table, the feedback procedure for learner writing, and the practice load per round.
Not here: the round loop and commands (SKILL.md, Round loop); sentence and paragraph templates (references/sentence-analysis.md); the error log, review queue and exercise status (references/progress-and-assessment.md); scores for learner writing (references/writing-quality-scorecard.md §7); stage exams and Show answer (references/exam-and-assessment.md).

## 1. Near-synonym discrimination

### 1.1 Choosing contrasts

Choose one to three word groups that the round's sentences use or invite. For each word, teach meaning, part of speech, grammar pattern, collocations, register and claim strength, and the contexts where the words are not interchangeable. Explain why the author's word fits this sentence. Common groups:

| Group | What separates the words |
|---|---|
| show / demonstrate / reveal | *show* is the general verb and also describes what a material displays ("mutants show a phenotype"); *demonstrate* presents evidence as establishing a claim; *reveal* presents a finding as uncovering something not visible before, often with an analysis or method as subject |
| suggest / indicate / imply | They differ in the kind of link, not on a fixed strength scale. *suggest* offers an inference the evidence supports but does not settle; *indicate* presents data as pointing to a conclusion; *imply* says a conclusion follows from something without being stated. Hedges (*may*, *strongly*) and the evidence itself move each one, and field conventions differ |
| identify / determine / characterize | finding which entity is involved; establishing a value, structure or outcome; describing the properties of something already identified |
| increase / enhance / expand | a neutral rise in amount (also intransitive: "levels increased"); intensifying a property or effect (transitive only); growth in size, range or number of members |
| affect / influence / regulate | an effect of unstated direction or mechanism; shaping an outcome, often indirectly or partly; controlled adjustment of a process, which needs evidence of a mechanism |
| cause / result in / contribute to | direct causation, which needs strong causal evidence; an outcome relation (*X results in Y*, but *Y results from X*); one cause among several |
| observe / detect / discover | recording what was seen in the study; finding that something is present with a method; finding something not previously known, a strong novelty claim |
| significant / substantial / marked | in research writing *significant* implies a statistical test, so use it only with one; *substantial* and *marked* describe size without implying a test |

Do not teach any group as a rigid universal ranking. The surrounding evidence and the sentence's construction decide the choice.

### 1.2 Teaching sequence

1. Show the source word in its sentence, with the locator.
2. Explain its meaning, grammar pattern, collocations and claim strength here.
3. Compare two or more plausible alternatives and say why each fits less well in this sentence.
4. Set the item (§1.3) and wait for the learner's answer.
5. Explain the key and each distractor.
6. Log repeated confusions under `LEX` (references/progress-and-assessment.md §4).

### 1.3 Item design

- Each item has exactly one best answer. Something inside the item decides it: grammar, collocation, register, or the strength of evidence that the item states. Outside knowledge never decides it.
- Each distractor is a real learner confusion with a reason it fails that can be stated. Avoid options that no learner would choose.
- Write the key and the reason for each distractor before showing the item (§6.3).

【教学示例·非原文 Teaching example】 Expression of the reporter gene ______ threefold after heat treatment.

A. increased  B. enhanced  C. raised  D. expanded

Key, kept for the tutor and never shown before the attempt: A. *increased* is intransitive and neutral for a measured amount. B fails because *enhance* is transitive and intensifies a property; it needs an object or the passive. C fails because *raise* is transitive; the intransitive verb is *rise*, past tense *rose*. D fails because *expand* means growth in size or range, not a rise in amount.

## 2. Chinglish diagnosis and revision

### 2.1 Item design

Each item has three parts:

1. A statement in the learner's first language about the round's scientific content.
2. A deliberately awkward or error-prone English draft, labeled 【教学示例·非原文 Teaching example】. Plant realistic errors, preferring the learner's recurring error codes (§4).
3. An instruction to revise the draft independently and say briefly what was changed and why.

Prepare the intended revision and the list of planted errors as the key before the item is shown. "Chinglish" names the case of a Chinese first language; for another first language, plant the transfer errors typical of that language. A draft contains scientific errors only when they are planted on purpose and coded `SCI` or `HDG`.

### 2.2 Feedback and revision levels

Give feedback by the procedure in §5 (dimension bands only, no total). Where useful, offer up to three versions:

| Version | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Basic correct | Fixes grammar and meaning while staying direct |
| Recommended academic | Natural, precise and suitable for a research manuscript |
| Advanced alternative | A different mature structure, adding no unsupported claim |

Longer or more complex is not automatically better. Say when the basic version is stronger because it is clearer.

## 3. Active imitation and writing transfer

### 3.1 Sentence-level imitation

1. Select one or two useful patterns from the round.
2. Explain their syntax, key vocabulary, rhetorical purpose and logic.
3. Abstract the structure with X / Y / Z placeholders.
4. Give a new research scenario (§6.3) that needs a structural decision, not word-for-word substitution: for example, add the comparison the source left implicit, or choose a reporting verb that matches the stated evidence.
5. Ask the learner to write independently. Do not show a complete model answer first.
6. Give feedback by §5 (dimension bands only), with a recommended revision, and explain how it relates to the source pattern without copying it.

Invented scenarios are teaching examples, not findings. Use placeholder names (gene X, protein Y, strain Z) and never attach invented results to a real named gene, organism study or paper, including the source paper.

### 3.2 Paragraph-level transfer

At the end of a subsection, ask for a short original paragraph that uses the subsection's patterns. When the subsection also ends a stage, the stage exam's writing task takes its place (references/exam-and-assessment.md §1). Give feedback by §5 (dimension bands and a total), and include:

- the learner's original version;
- issues and explanations;
- a corrected version and a recommended academic version;
- a comparison with the source pattern;
- strengths worth keeping;
- the next improvement target.

## 4. Error codes

Tag every error in learner writing with one code. The examples are short teaching examples, not source text.

| Code | Error type | 【教学示例·非原文 Teaching example】 |
|---|---|---|
| `ART` | Articles and determiners | ✗ Mutant showed reduced growth. → ✓ The mutant showed reduced growth. |
| `NUM` | Number, countability and agreement | ✗ These evidences suggest … → ✓ This evidence suggests … |
| `TNS` | Tense, aspect and voice | ✗ Cell death was occurred after 24 h. → ✓ Cell death occurred after 24 h. |
| `VPT` | Verb patterns and complements | ✗ This method allows to measure uptake. → ✓ This method allows us to measure uptake. |
| `PRP` | Prepositions | ✗ Cells were treated by 10 µM cycloheximide. → ✓ Cells were treated with 10 µM cycloheximide. |
| `COL` | Collocations | ✗ We made three independent experiments. → ✓ We performed three independent experiments. |
| `LEX` | Word choice and register, including near-synonym misuse | ✗ We checked the enzyme activity. → ✓ We measured the enzyme activity. |
| `ORD` | Word order and information structure | ✗ We also can detect the protein. → ✓ We can also detect the protein. |
| `LNK` | Cohesion and connectors | ✗ Although growth was reduced, but survival was unchanged. → ✓ Although growth was reduced, survival was unchanged. |
| `HDG` | Claim strength, too strong or too weak | ✗ This single knockdown proves that X is required for Y. → ✓ This single knockdown suggests that X is required for Y. |
| `TRM` | Terminology and naming conventions | ✗ escherichia coli → ✓ *Escherichia coli* |
| `RED` | Redundancy | ✗ Activity was completely abolished. → ✓ Activity was abolished. |
| `STR` | Sentence structure: run-on sentences, fragments, dangling modifiers | ✗ After adding the inhibitor, the cells stopped dividing. → ✓ After the inhibitor was added, the cells stopped dividing. |
| `PUN` | Punctuation and formatting | ✗ in 10 mM NaCl，pH 7.5 (full-width comma) → ✓ in 10 mM NaCl, pH 7.5 |
| `SCI` | Scientific reasoning errors: result confused with interpretation, causal overreach | ✗ X levels correlated with survival, so X causes survival. → ✓ X levels correlated with survival; whether X affects survival was not tested. |

Tagging rules:

- One code per error: the code whose fix resolves it. A comma splice or run-on sentence is `STR`, not `PUN`.
- Spelling slips have no code: correct them and mark them "spelling"; references/writing-quality-scorecard.md §2 says where they are scored.
- A wrong preposition is `PRP` even inside a fixed phrase; a wrong content word in a fixed phrase is `COL`.
- A near-synonym chosen wrongly for meaning or register is `LEX`; one that misstates claim strength is `HDG`. A wrong technical term or naming form is `TRM`.
- `HDG` is wording that overstates or understates the evidence while the reasoning is sound. `SCI` is reasoning that does not follow, however it is worded.
- `SCI` is counted separately from the language codes in feedback, the error log, checkpoint counts and exam reports. How the codes relate to the six writing dimensions is defined only in references/writing-quality-scorecard.md §2.

## 5. Learner writing feedback

Applies to Chinglish revisions, imitation sentences, paragraph tasks and exam writing tasks.

1. Quote the learner's text exactly. Do not correct it silently.
2. List language errors first: quoted span, code, fix and a one-line reason.
3. List scientific reasoning errors separately under `SCI`. Judge them only against the evidence the task gives, not against outside knowledge. A sentence can be correct English and poor reasoning, or sound reasoning in faulty English; report the two separately.
4. Report the six writing dimensions by their exact names: band, the quoted span that supports it, and the related codes. Mark a dimension that does not apply to the task N/A. Bands, N/A handling and the total are defined in references/writing-quality-scorecard.md §7 and are not repeated here.
5. Single-sentence practice (Chinglish revision, imitation) gets bands only, no total. Paragraph tasks and exam writing tasks get bands and a total. The five-part 1.2.0 learner rubric is retired.
6. Give the revision versions that help (§2.2), name strengths with quoted spans, and set the next target: the weakest dimension and the most frequent code.
7. Log each error (references/progress-and-assessment.md §4); repeated codes enter the review queue (references/progress-and-assessment.md §5).

```text
Learner text: "<exact quote>"
Language errors: "<span>" <code> → <fix>; <reason>
Reasoning errors (SCI): "<span>" → <what the evidence supports>
Academic vocabulary precision: <band> | "<span>" | <codes>
Syntax and sentence control: <band> | "<span>" | <codes>
Paragraph logic and argumentation: <band or N/A> | "<span>" | <codes>
Natural academic expression: <band> | "<span>" | <codes>
Scientific reasoning and claim calibration: <band> | "<span>" | <codes>
Clarity and concision: <band> | "<span>" | <codes>
Total (paragraph and exam tasks only): see references/writing-quality-scorecard.md §7
Next target: <weakest dimension>; <most frequent code>
```

## 6. Practice load and pacing

### 6.1 Items per round

- End every round with one item for each mechanism: near-synonym discrimination (§1), Chinglish diagnosis and revision (§2) and imitation (§3).
- Add one more item. Take it from the review queue (references/progress-and-assessment.md §5) when the queue is not empty; otherwise target the mechanism where the learner is weakest. While there is no learner data yet (normally the first round), there is no extra item.
- 强化 / Intensive practice: give 2–3 items per mechanism for the current round.
- Place the items after the round's sentence lessons and any paragraph analysis. Do not interrupt each sentence with a quiz.
- Show no answers before the learner attempts the items. If the learner asks for an answer first, follow references/exam-and-assessment.md §5.

### 6.2 Pacing

- Raise difficulty only after the learner shows mastery.
- If the learner is overloaded, make the items shorter or easier rather than dropping a mechanism; all three run every round.
- Bring recurring errors back in later rounds through the review queue.
- Mark an item the learner chooses not to answer as `skipped`; it does not count as mastered.
- An item left unanswered when the learner moves on (for example by saying 继续) stays `pending` in the checkpoint; the learner can still answer it, and it becomes `skipped` only when the learner declines it.

### 6.3 Practice contexts and answer keys

- Build new scenarios from the learner profile (references/progress-and-assessment.md §2): the learner's field, study object and methods. When the profile is unknown, use general life-science scenarios.
- Label every invented sentence and scenario 【教学示例·非原文 Teaching example】 and use placeholder names (§3.1). Never present the source paper's findings, or invented results, as new findings.
- Write the key before showing an item: the best answer and the reason each distractor fails, or, for revision and imitation, the intended revision and the planted errors. After the learner answers, change the key only if it is shown to be wrong, and say so.
