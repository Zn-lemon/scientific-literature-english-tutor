# Stage examination and diagnostic reporting

Scope: when to examine, exam construction (the competency blueprint), the answer key first, scoring with bands and readiness decisions, the weak-competency rule, Show answer, the report format and the comprehensive assessment.
Not here: round practice items and their load (references/training-mechanisms.md §6); the six writing dimensions and how learner writing is scored (references/writing-quality-scorecard.md §7); the checkpoint, error log and review queue (references/progress-and-assessment.md).

## 1. When to examine

Run a formal exam after the learner completes a stage of one paper. A stage is normally the Abstract, the Introduction, each substantial Results subsection, the Discussion, or the Conclusions. A stage the learner defines takes precedence.

- Announce that the stage is complete and that an exam follows.
- The exam's writing task replaces the paragraph task that would have closed the stage (references/training-mechanisms.md §3.2).
- A stage exam is separate from round practice. The shorter 测试 / Test command checks material already studied with a few items and no formal report.

## 2. Exam construction

Default: 8–12 items plus one short writing task. Scale down for a short stage. Use only source content from the studied stage and language patterns already taught.

### 2.1 Blueprint

| Competency | Points | What is assessed |
|---|---:|---|
| Reading comprehension and argument structure | 20 | Main claim, evidence, sentence and paragraph function, result versus inference |
| Academic vocabulary and collocations | 15 | Meaning in context, word choice, natural combinations |
| Near-synonym discrimination | 10 | Precision, register and evidence strength |
| Grammar and sentence parsing | 15 | Clause structure, modifiers, non-finite forms, syntax |
| Translation and semantic fidelity | 10 | Accurate meaning, logic and qualification |
| Chinglish diagnosis and revision | 15 | Grammar, collocation, naturalness, scientific precision |
| Active imitation / short writing | 15 | Transfer of learned patterns into original writing |
| **Total** | **100** | |

The short writing task is the Active imitation / short writing item and carries its 15 points (§4).

Change the weighting only when a competency is genuinely absent from the stage, and state the adjustment in the exam and in the report.

### 2.2 Item rules

- Mix item types across the competencies.
- Include at least one item that asks the learner to tell the author's observation, result, interpretation and hypothesis apart.
- Include at least one productive task: a sentence or a short paragraph.
- Add no facts that are not in the paper. A comprehension item's correct answer must be supported by the studied section, with its locator.
- Keep language competence separate from subject knowledge. Do not penalise a learner for rejecting an inference the paper does not support.
- Cover review-queue items and recurring error codes first (references/progress-and-assessment.md §5).
- Show each item with its competency and points, and with no answer or hint.

## 3. Answer key first

1. Before showing the exam, write the answer key and the item map: for each item, its competency, its points, the correct answer and the grading basis (the source locator or the rule taught).
2. Save the key to a file when the workspace allows it; otherwise keep it unshown.
3. After the exam has been shown, change the key only if it is shown to be wrong. Record the change and the reason in the report.
4. Reveal answers, hints and model responses only after the learner submits, except under Show answer (§5).

## 4. Scoring, bands and readiness

Grade each item against the key. Score the writing task on the six writing dimensions and convert it to its 15 points as set out in references/writing-quality-scorecard.md §7.

| Total | Band | Readiness decision |
|---|---|---|
| 90–100 | Strong mastery | `advance`, with spaced review |
| 80–89 | Good command | `advance`, with targeted review of weak areas |
| 70–79 | Developing | `reinforce-then-advance`: a short reinforcement set on the weak skills, then the next stage |
| 60–69 | Needs reinforcement | `review-and-retest`: revisit the relevant sentences, repeat selected exercises, then retest |
| below 60 | Re-teach recommended | `re-teach`: explain the main gaps, give a focused remedial lesson, then a new equivalent exam |

Weak-competency rule: if any competency scores below 60% of its points, an `advance` decision becomes `reinforce-then-advance`, whatever the total; `review-and-retest` and `re-teach` stay as they are.

Scores are diagnostic, not punitive. The bands and the 60% rule are internal teaching rules, not validated test cut-offs; do not claim statistical validity or equivalence to a standardised test.

Use the results to adjust later rounds:

- weak vocabulary → more contextual vocabulary and collocation retrieval;
- weak grammar → revisit clause parsing and sentence reconstruction;
- weak translation → practise semantic units and logical connectors;
- weak Chinglish revision → target the learner's repeated error codes;
- weak imitation or writing → smaller sentence-level transfer tasks before paragraph tasks;
- strong performance → less repeated explanation and more independent writing.

Record the score, band, decision and next target in the checkpoint (references/progress-and-assessment.md §1).

## 5. Show answer

When the learner asks to see an answer before attempting the item (显示答案 / Show answer), show it. Record the item as `shown-without-attempt`: it does not count as mastered, it earns no points in an exam, and it enters the review queue (references/progress-and-assessment.md §5). The same rule applies to round practice items.

## 6. Report format

Write the report after grading. Be specific: name the words, collocations, grammar patterns, error codes or logic problems involved, never generic advice such as "improve vocabulary".

```text
Stage Learning Assessment Report
Paper: <title> | Stage: <stage> | Date: <date, if known>
Overall score: <n>/100 | Band: <band> | Readiness decision: <token>
Weighting adjustments: <none, or what changed and why>
Key changes after the exam was shown: <none, or what and why>

Score by competency (score / max | evidence):
- Reading comprehension and argument structure: /20 | ...
- Academic vocabulary and collocations: /15 | ...
- Near-synonym discrimination: /10 | ...
- Grammar and sentence parsing: /15 | ...
- Translation and semantic fidelity: /10 | ...
- Chinglish diagnosis and revision: /15 | ...
- Active imitation / short writing: /15 | ...

Findings
- Correct answers and explanations for missed items:
- Writing task: original, corrected version, recommended academic version, dimension bands:
- Strengths demonstrated:
- Recurring error codes, and whether they match the error log:
- Scientific-meaning or evidence-calibration issues:
- Items shown without attempt:

Improvement plan: three prioritized actions, each naming the exact item to work on.
Next step: the first action that the readiness decision calls for.
```

## 7. Comprehensive assessment

综合考核 / Comprehensive assessment covers several completed stages or the whole paper. Use the same blueprint (§2.1), the answer key first (§3), the same scoring and readiness rules (§4) and the same report format (§6), with the item count scaled to the material covered. At the end of the paper, add the cumulative score trend across stages to the report and to the final learning portfolio (references/progress-and-assessment.md §6).
